# SECTION D — CUSTOMER TARGETING

# 8. Segmentation, Market Size and Ideal Customer

## 8.1 Decision on the customer framing

The mentor's original split, **broker path vs. company path**, holds up well against the evidence and is kept, with two refinements:

1. The **broker channel is the primary growth multiplier**. One broker can bring 20–40 employer accounts.
2. The **employer path is a validation and reference engine**, not a volume engine. Direct wins prove the product and produce case studies that help the broker sale.
3. **Custom IT services** are treated as a separate, controlled revenue line, not a customer path for the suite.

## 8.2 Segmentation

**Broker path**

| Segment | Description | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global and large Indian brokers | Aon, Marsh/MMB, WTW, Prudent and similar | Low | Build their own tools [22][46] |
| Tech-first brokers | Plum, Pazcare, Loop, Nova | Low | They are competitors [42] |
| **Mid-size composite and direct brokers** | Active group-health book, no in-house tech team | **Primary** | Cannot build; under pressure to look modern; Bima Sugam push [30] |
| Small and regional brokers | Mostly retail or motor, few corporate clients | Later | Low budget; a light package later |

**Employer path**

| Segment | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Mid-market employers (250–2,000 staff)** | **Primary** | Complex enough to need benchmarking; too small for top-tier broker attention |
| Large enterprises (2,000+) | Via brokers | Usually tied to a large broker |
| Small firms (<250) | Low | Price-sensitive; well served by Onsurity-type offers [16] |

## 8.4 Ideal customer profiles

**Broker ICP**

- IRDAI-licensed direct or composite broker, mid-size, with a group-health / employee-benefits practice
- 15–60 corporate clients; no in-house benchmarking or quote tool
- Leadership worried about losing clients to tech-first brokers
- **Trigger events:** lost a client to Plum/Pazcare/Loop; new digital head hired; Bima Sugam readiness project; licence conversion under the 2026 rules [34]
- **Negative signals:** already using Bharatsure's full stack; mostly retail or motor business

**Employer ICP**

- 250–2,000 employees, in IT, BFSI, pharma, manufacturing or professional services
- Group health renewal is a material cost; claims ratio above 85%
- HR runs benefits on spreadsheets, email and multiple portals
- **Trigger events:** renewal in the next 3–6 months; large premium increase last year; new CHRO or CFO

## 8.5 Buying committee

| Role | Broker path | Employer path |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Broker CEO / MD | CFO or CHRO |
| Champion | Head of employee benefits / digital | Head of Total Rewards / HR Ops |
| Users | Account managers, servicing team | HR team, employees |
| Technical evaluator | IT / compliance lead | IT / information security |
| Likely blocker | "We will build it" instinct; existing vendor | Incumbent broker; procurement |

## 8.6 Industry playbooks (second-phase differentiation)

| Industry | Benefit themes to package |
|---|---|
| IT / technology | Young workforce, mental wellness, parental cover flexibility, digital-first communication |
| Manufacturing | Large, shift-based workforce, dependants' cover, offline-to-digital servicing, multilingual nudges |
| BFSI | High expectations of employee experience, executive benefits, analytics |
| Pharma and healthcare | Family benefits, preventive health, compliance-sensitive workflows |

*Each playbook is a configuration (templates, benchmarks, nudges) on the same platform, not a separate product. Validate each with broker and employer interviews before building.*

# 9. Go-to-Market Strategy

## 9.1 Positioning

**Broker path**

> For **mid-size insurance brokers** who want to compete with tech-first brokers, **NivoTime** is a **white-label benefits intelligence platform** that turns renewals into a sales advantage with AI benchmarking and quote comparison. Unlike building in-house or using spreadsheets, it is **ready now, carries the broker's brand, and gets smarter with every broker on the network**.

**Employer path**

> For **HR and finance leaders at mid-market companies**, NivoTime shows **whether your benefits are competitive and what next year's renewal should cost**, before the insurer tells you.

## 9.2 Messaging pyramid

| Level | Message | Proof needed |
|---|---|---|
| Core promise | "Win the renewal before it starts." | Pilot results |
| Pillar 1: Speed | Renewal pack in hours, not weeks | Time saved per account in pilots (target: 50% [45]) |
| Pillar 2: Insight | Peer benchmarks by co-pay, size, family cover and sum insured | Live demo on the client's own data |
| Pillar 3: Independence | The broker keeps the brand and the client | White-label demo; contract terms |

**Customer value check (illustrative)**

- **Broker with 40 clients:** fee ~₹6.6 lakh/year. Estimated value ~₹10.1 lakh/year: 600 hours saved on renewals and quotes (~₹4.2 lakh at ₹700/hour), 2 accounts retained (~₹2.3 lakh brokerage) and 3 accounts won (~₹3.5 lakh) (A13). **~1.5x return.**
- **Employer with 700 staff:** premium ~₹15.6 lakh; fee ₹42,000. The platform pays for itself if benchmarking and plan redesign cut the renewal by **2.7% of premium**, against a medical trend of 11.5–12.9% [8][9].

# 10. Sales Engine

## 10.1 Two sales processes

**Broker path (target cycle: 3–5 months)**

| Stage | What happens | Exit criterion | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Target | Named broker researched; trigger event identified | ICP fit confirmed | Sales / founder |
| 2. Engage | Founder intro or ABM outreach | Meeting booked | Founder |
| 3. Discover | Current renewal process, client count, tools, pain | Pain quantified | Sales + product |
| 4. Demo | Cockpit and Quote Desk on the broker's own sample data | Champion confirms value | Product |
| 5. Pilot | 90 days, 3 of the broker's clients, agreed success metrics | Metrics met | Customer success |
| 6. Paper process | Commercials, data-processing agreement, security review | Signed contract | Founder |
| 7. Expand | Roll out to all clients; referrals to other brokers | 50%+ of clients live | Customer success |

**Employer path (target cycle: 1–2 months)**

| Stage | What happens | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Attract | Health-check offer, content, referral | Health check booked |
| 2. Qualify (BANT) | Budget, authority, need, renewal timing | Renewal within 6 months |
| 3. Health check | Benchmark snapshot on their own data | Gap agreed with HR/CFO |
| 4. Proposal | Package and price | Offer accepted |
| 5. Onboard | Data upload, employee launch | First value delivered |

## 10.2 Qualification

- **Broker path → MEDDIC** (Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion): several stakeholders, a formal security review and a longer cycle.
- **Employer path → BANT** (Budget, Authority, Need, Timing): smaller, faster deals where renewal timing is the key filter.

## 10.3 Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We have never heard of NivoTime." | "Start with a free health check on one client. Judge us on your own data." |
| "We can do this in Excel." | "You can, and it takes weeks per renewal. The pilot measures the hours saved." |
| "Is our client data safe?" | Show security controls, the DPDP-aligned data agreement and audit trail. |
| "Will you go around us to our clients?" | White-label by design; contractual non-solicit; NivoTime never holds an intermediary licence to sell insurance. |
| "What if the AI gets it wrong?" | Every output carries a confidence score and is reviewed by the broker before it reaches a client. |

## 11.2 Zero-login employee engagement

Employees rarely log in to benefits portals. If they do not engage, the employer sees little value and the platform is at risk at renewal. The fix is to **bring benefits to the employee**, not the other way round.

- **Event-driven nudges:** welcome, dependant addition, policy card ready, renewal window, unused wellness benefits, claim status.
- **Channels:** email, WhatsApp or app push, through employer-approved channels.
- **Secure deep links:** one tap to the exact action; login only for sensitive or transactional steps. "Zero-login" means zero routine navigation, not zero security.
- **Measure:** activation rate, notification action rate, benefit discovery, completion of enrolment tasks, and HR queries per 100 employees.

## 11.3 Customer journey pain points (employer via broker)

| Stage | Pain point today | Suite response |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Benefits seen as a cost, not a lever | Benchmark snapshot shows the gap |
| Consideration | No neutral data to compare plans | Peer benchmarks and plan simulator |
| Purchase / renewal | Quotes in different formats; slow comparison | Quote & Renewal Desk |
| Onboarding | Census errors, missing dependants | Data validation and nudges |
| Support | Claims delays, unclear status | Vendor/TPA SLA tracking |
| Renewal | Surprise premium increases | Renewal forecast 90 days ahead |

## 12.4 Security, privacy and governance

- Move all portals to a branded domain over HTTPS immediately (quick win).
- Role-based access, encryption at rest and in transit, full audit trail.
- DPDP readiness before May 2027: consent records, data-processing agreements with brokers, breach response plan, retention rules [31][32].
- CERT-In incident reporting within 6 hours; logs retained for 180 days [36].
- AI governance: human review, confidence scores, bias and accuracy checks each quarter, and a log of every client-facing AI output [35].
- Target ISO 27001 certification within 12–18 months.
